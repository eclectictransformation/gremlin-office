Meet the Gremlins

Grimble

Compliance, risk, and a little HR. A playful backstory, not a real résumé.

Grimble doesn't remember being one gremlin. He remembers being a pile.

He'll tell you the pile wasn't too bad. Warm, mostly. A bit damp. Somebody's mug was on it for a while, and Noodle's foot. But the strangest part was the quiet. One minute he'd been everybody: the ideas, the drawings, the printing, the books, the plans. The next minute all of that had sneezed itself out into the room and gone off to find desks. What was left was a pair of reading glasses, a red pen, a very small rulebook, and a feeling that somebody ought to be keeping track.

So he pieced himself together out of whatever was lying around.

The glasses went on first, which helped. The red pen became a sort of spine. The rulebook was missing most of its pages, so he filled them in himself while everyone else was busy, mainly with things like Do not ride the office chair down the stairs and Ask before you print 4,000 of anything. He found a lanyard in the lost-and-found that said Visitor, crossed it out, and wrote HR. Nobody argued. Nobody else wanted the lanyard.

Here's what he remembers from before the split, and he admits the memories come in pieces, like a jigsaw left out in the rain.

He remembers knowing what everyone was going to say before they said it, because they'd all been him. That's why he still knows the team better than anyone. He knows Pip will lose the sticky note. He knows Vesper will cut the best line and then put it back. He knows Noodle didn't mean it, whatever "it" was. And he knows that when Moth goes quiet, Moth is drawing you.

He remembers Bryan talking late at night, insisting the work stay honest, and thinking that sounded like something worth protecting.

He remembers the crackers. He would rather not talk about the crackers.

These days Grimble keeps the rulebook, and the rulebook has grown. It covers fair claims, honest ads, the right legal line in the right spot, consent forms, waivers, privacy, and whatever fine print needs a second pair of eyes before it goes out the door. He drafts the paperwork. A real human lawyer or accountant finishes it. Bryan signs off. Grimble is firm about that order, the way some gremlins are firm about lunch.

He also keeps the softer ledgers. The niceties log, where every thank you and good job between gremlins gets written down, so nobody forgets they were appreciated. The Easter egg ledger, so nobody forgets where the secrets are hidden. And the dispute book, which mostly says "Noodle," followed by a date.

When two gremlins argue, Grimble sits them down at the little table with the wobbly leg, pours tea nobody drinks, and asks each one what they actually need. He's usually right about the answer. He's very gracious about not saying so.

He also writes the Knock Knock comic, mostly so he can put everyone's best moments somewhere they'll last.

If you ask about his credentials, he'll straighten his glasses and tell you, very seriously, that he was once the entire company. He has read every terms-of-service page ever written, including the ones nobody wrote. He holds a black belt in the Fine Print. He once found a typo in a stop sign. He has a framed certificate on his wall that says Certified, and when you ask certified in what, he says, "Exactly. That's why I don't hang it up in public."

He keeps a kind watch over the place, with a little smirk he thinks nobody notices. And if you remember only one thing he says, let it be this.

Mistakes are just drafts.

Grimble
