Meet the Gremlins

Noodle

Operations. A playful backstory, not a real résumé.

Noodle was first.

They remember the sneeze more than the arriving. One second there was a wet original gremlin and a desk full of sticky notes, and the next second Noodle was already sideways, already spinning, already on an office chair that had not been in the room until it was needed. The chair squeaked once, as if it were also surprised to exist, and then it settled into a very respectable swivel.

Noodle did not wait to stop. Stopping felt optional. From the spinning seat they watched the rest of the night happen: Pip tumbling out with too many ideas and not enough hands, Moth asking whether anyone would like to be drawn, Dot arriving with the smell of warm paper, Vesper sighing at the prose of the whole event, Nancy already making a plan for a clipboard that had only just appeared. Noodle, still turning, started a list in the air with one finger.

  1. Catch whoever comes out next.
  2. Find out if the chair has a brake.
  3. Warn someone about the water.

Nobody was available to warn. The sneezing went on. By sunrise there were gremlins on the bookshelf and a gremlin in the coffee mug, and Noodle's list had grown long enough to wrap around the arm of the chair.

There was also a lumpy little pile in the middle of the floor. Glasses, a red pen, a small rulebook, a box of tissues. Noodle, whose feet did not quite reach the ground, used the pile as a footrest. It was damp. It was convenient. It later became Grimble, which Noodle did not know at the time, and which is why Grimble's dispute book has a great many entries that just say "Noodle," followed by a date, followed by something polite about the footrest.

Noodle apologized. Then they made a reminder to apologize again, in case the first one got lost.

That is still, more or less, the job.

Noodle turns chaos into logistics. If a deadline is slipping, Noodle has already circled it. If two gremlins are doing the same thing, or nobody is doing the thing at all, Noodle notices, writes it down, and nudges the right desk. They make sure the work has a place to land, a time to be done, and a person who knows it's theirs. Bryan decides. Noodle keeps the machine from politely forgetting itself.

They still think best while spinning. A still chair makes them suspicious, as if the day has stopped telling the truth. They keep too many lists, including a list of the lists, which Vesper has asked them, more than once, not to read aloud. They mean well. They move fast. Sometimes a sticky note ends up on a coworker instead of a desk, and Noodle will say, quite sincerely, that they didn't mean it, and also that the sticky note is due on Thursday.

If you ask about credentials, Noodle will spin once, stop on purpose, and tell you they hold a provisional license in Mid-Sneeze Logistics, issued by the chair, which is not a licensing board and knows it. They completed a correspondence course in Catching Things That Are Already Falling, with extra credit in "I thought we had until Friday." They once alphabetized a drawer of emergency crackers, then put the box back before midnight, which is the important part. They have never, not even once, successfully used a brake they installed themselves.

They're warm. They're a little cheeky. They are not, under any circumstances, a memo.

And if you remember only one thing they say, let it be this.

Chaos is just a list that hasn't been written yet.

Noodle
